= Appendix: Further Reading

This book doesn't try to be everything. These are other resources worth
your time, listed honestly along with what they're good for. None of their
text is reproduced in this book — see `CONTRIBUTING.md` for why.

== Books

https://www.braveclojure.com/[Clojure for the Brave and True], by Daniel
Higginbotham :: A full language and concurrency book with a distinctive,
funny voice. Free to read online (not open-licensed — see its own site
for terms). Doesn't cover deploying to production.

https://pragprog.com/titles/vmclojeco/clojure-applied/[Clojure Applied],
by Ben Vandgrift and Alex Miller :: Commercial. The closest existing
precedent to this book's goals — its later chapters treat testing,
integration, and deployment as first-class concerns rather than an
afterthought.

https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/living-clojure/9781491909270/[Living
Clojure], by Carin Meier :: Commercial. A guided, exercise-driven tour of
the language.

== Guides and curricula

https://practical.li/clojure/[Practicalli Clojure], by John "Jr0cket"
Stevenson :: Extensive, actively maintained, CC BY-SA 4.0 licensed
coverage of the CLI/REPL workflow, testing, spec, and CI. Its own
deployment-focused guide was archived rather than kept current — worth
knowing going in.

https://github.com/ClojureBridge/curriculum[ClojureBridge Curriculum] ::
CC BY 4.0 licensed workshop curriculum aimed at first-time programmers,
good source of exercise ideas for early chapters.

https://exercism.org/tracks/clojure[Exercism's Clojure Track] :: Free,
mentored, exercise-by-exercise practice — a good companion once you're
past this book's early chapters and want more reps.

== Also worth knowing about

https://github.com/functional-koans/clojure-koans[Clojure Koans] ::
Fill-in-the-blank exercises covering language syntax.

See `plan.md` section 2.3 for the fuller comparison (scope, license, and
what each does and doesn't cover) that informed this book's own scope.
